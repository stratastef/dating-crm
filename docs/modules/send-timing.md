# Send timing

All in `app/timing.py` (ADR 0008). Every draft has a **send time** (`drafts.send_at`) and a one-line `timing_reason`. For autopilot it is when the **planned send** goes out; for the user's own (copilot) drafts it is when the draft is best sent, from the same formula and bounds, until the user picks Send at (a **scheduled send**, see "Drafts"), which uses their time as given.

- **The formula:** `formula(db, user_id, card_id, now) -> timedelta`, the wait from `now`:
  - **pace:** the median of the match's last 5 reply gaps (from one of the user's messages to their answer), held to 1 minute–6 hours; 45 minutes when no gap is known;
  - times **engagement** of their messages since the user's last one: a question 0.75, 120 characters or more 0.8, under 20 characters with no question 1.25 (a question and length multiply);
  - times the **hour** of `now` in the user's time zone (`user_zone()`): 18:00–24:00 0.8, 09:00–18:00 1.2, otherwise 1;
  - counted from their last message, so the time since it is already waited (never below 0).
- **The model's adjustment:** `draft_for()` puts the formula's wait in the prompt (`<send_timing>`). Claude's `DraftReply` gives `adjustment` (a fraction of the wait: `-0.5` halves it, `0.5` adds half, default 0) and `timing_reason`, in the same call that writes the draft and the topic.
- **The hard bounds:** `send_time(db, user_id, card_id, wait, adjustment, now) -> datetime`, in this order:
  1. the adjustment is clamped to half either way (`MAX_ADJUSTMENT`);
  2. at least 2 minutes from `now` when the match is **live** (their last message is at most 5 minutes old), otherwise at least 10 minutes;
  3. at most 12 hours;
  4. the user's **night window**, last: `night_rule(db, user_id, card_id, at, now)`. A live match gets the reply at the live pace, night or not. Otherwise a time inside the window moves to the window's end plus 1–15 random minutes; when that is more than 12 hours from `now`, it moves to 1–15 random minutes before the window starts instead, if that is still at least 10 minutes from `now`, and only failing that to the window's end. The owner decided both on 2026-11-25 (ADR 0008, "Amended").
- **Openers:** an opener has no conversation to time it. `opener_time(db, user_id, synced)` picks a random time 30 minutes to 6 hours after the match synced (`OPENER_WINDOW`, from the card's `created_at`), moves it out of the night window with `outside_night()` (the window's end plus 1–15 random minutes: no live match, no ceiling), then moves it on past each of the user's planned openers closer than 10 minutes (`OPENER_GAP`). The night window and the gap win over the 6 hours, so a burst of new matches spreads out later. It locks the user's row, so two workers take turns. The opener's `timing_reason` is `OPENER_REASON` ("a while after you matched"). Only a match new since the app was connected gets an opener (see "Chat autopilot").
- **Night window setting:** `night_window_start` and `night_window_end` on `GET`/`PATCH /settings`: whole hours 0–23 in the user's time zone, 1 and 7 by default. A start after the end wraps past midnight (22 to 9); equal hours make no window. Other hours are `422`.
- **Planned sends:** the `chat_reply` job queues its reply with `queue_send(..., unattended=True, run_at=draft.send_at)`. When it runs, the `send_draft` job first asks `night_rule()` again for the moment it would send (the send time, or now if later): the user may have moved the night window or changed time zone since. A time it moves is the draft's new `send_at`, with a new `send_draft` job; the draft stays a planned send. An opener moved this way is not spaced from the other openers again. Then the job asks `unattended_block()`, so a pause, the AI budget, a guardrail hit or a handover since then keeps the draft `pending`, in Review. A scheduled send (the user's time) is never moved. The SQL condition for "this draft is a planned send" is `PLANNED` in `app/drafts.py`: `approved` with a queued, unattended `send_draft` job.
- **A new message on the card**, from either side (`insert_message()`), calls `drop_planned_sends()` in `app/send.py`: each planned send on the card is taken back with `cancel_send()`, `dismissed`, and its calendar holds are released. If the match wrote, chat autopilot then plans a new reply; if the user wrote (an opener of their own, say), nothing replaces it. One the worker is already sending goes out.
- **Where it shows:** the card summary's `planned_send` (a `Draft`, with `send_at` and `timing_reason`), and the Pipeline: a card with a planned send is Handled by autopilot with `autopilot_activity` `planned`.
- **Send now and Hold** act on a planned send (see "Drafts"). `POST /drafts/{id}/send-now` takes it back with `cancel_send()` and queues it again with `queue_send()` to run at once, as the user's send (not unattended), so nothing goes out at the old send time. `POST /drafts/{id}/hold` is `undo-send`: `cancel_send()` alone, so the draft is `pending`, in Review. Its calendar holds stay: it still offers the same slots.
- **Limits:** the weights are a first guess, not fitted to outcomes. The send time is planned when the draft is written: a copilot draft read hours later shows a time that has passed. Not run against the real Claude.
